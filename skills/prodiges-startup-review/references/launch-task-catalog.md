# SaaS launch task catalog

Use this as a selectable library. Instantiate relevant tasks, adapt them to the startup, and retain IDs L01–L60. Counts and timelines are illustrative. Source: the Prodiges SaaS Launch Task Plan created on 2026-11-21. No startup status is inherited from a template.

Priority applies to the relevant milestone. Post-launch P0 work does not block the initial launch. Research and feasibility can overlap. Offer and pricing inform sales materials; delivery, billing, support, and measurement precede the paid pilot.

## 1 Launch scope and ownership

- **L01 · Write the launch brief (P0).** Completion: Record the problem, intended outcome, initial market, product hypothesis, and what this launch must prove in one page.

- **L02 · Choose an initial customer segment (P0).** Completion: Select one segment, geography, and primary use case. Identify the end user, payer, and any buyer or approver; mark these choices as hypotheses until interviews confirm them.

- **L03 · Set launch success criteria (P0).** Completion: Define what counts as qualified interest, activation, payment, and repeat use. Set explicit 30-day targets and measurement windows appropriate to the product.

- **L04 · Assign owners and a launch budget (P0).** Completion: Name one accountable person for product, customer discovery, commercial decisions, and operations. Set available time, spending limits, and a provisional milestone calendar.

- **L05 · Create the task board and decision log (P1).** Completion: Use Backlog, Ready, In progress, Blocked, and Done. Track assumptions, evidence, decisions, and unresolved questions; every active task has one owner and a next action.

## 2 Customers and problem validation

- **L06 · Draft the ideal customer profile (P0).** Completion: Describe the segment, context, triggering event, urgent problem, existing workaround, purchase constraints, and explicit exclusions. For B2B, distinguish user, champion, and budget owner.

- **L07 · Recruit customer interviews (P0).** Completion: Build a relevant interview list and book an initial 10–15 conversations. Include people outside your immediate network and record how each person fits the target segment.

- **L08 · Interview customers about actual behavior (P0).** Completion: Ask about the last occurrence of the problem, its consequences, current spending, alternatives tried, and how a purchase happens. Save notes and permission for any recording.

- **L09 · Map the current customer workflow (P0).** Completion: Document steps, tools, time spent, failure points, and consequences. Distinguish observed facts from estimates and extract the customer's own language.

- **L10 · Choose the problem to solve first (P0).** Completion: Summarize recurring evidence, contradictory findings, and the proposed solution hypothesis. Recruit interested testers and record concrete commitments such as a booked session or time allocated to a pilot.

## 3 Competition and market opportunity

- **L11 · Identify competitors and substitutes (P0).** Completion: List 5–10 relevant alternatives across direct competitors, adjacent products, agencies or freelancers, spreadsheets, and doing nothing.

- **L12 · Build a sourced competitor matrix (P0).** Completion: Compare target users, promise, core workflow, pricing unit, actual price, billing term, plan limits, trial, onboarding, support, and strengths. Save source URLs and observation dates.

- **L13 · Investigate unmet needs and switching barriers (P1).** Completion: Review customer comments and test accessible alternatives. Separate isolated complaints from repeated patterns; record migration effort, learning costs, integrations, and reasons customers stay.

- **L14 · Estimate the reachable launch market (P1).** Completion: Estimate reachable customers × annual revenue per customer. For a nonrecurring offer, calculate annual revenue per customer from expected purchases per year × price per purchase. Show assumptions, sources, and low/base/high scenarios; distinguish reachable customers from the total market.

- **L15 · Choose a defensible initial difference (P0).** Completion: Write the specific reason the chosen customer would switch or buy. Test whether that difference matters in interviews; avoid using AI alone as the reason to choose the product.

## 4 Positioning and the offer

- **L16 · Write the positioning statement (P0).** Completion: Specify whom the product serves, the problem, the promised outcome, the product category, and the meaningful difference from the customer's current alternative.

- **L17 · Define the core offer (P0).** Completion: Describe the outcome, included capabilities, usage boundaries, onboarding, support, eligibility, exclusions, and any customer effort needed to obtain the result.

- **L18 · Define the customer buying journey (P0).** Completion: Choose self-service, assisted purchase, or a sales-led pilot. Map discovery, evaluation, purchase, first value, repeat use, and renewal or repurchase.

- **L19 · Design the pilot or founding customer offer (P0).** Completion: Set pilot scope, duration, price hypothesis, responsibilities, success measures, exit terms, and what happens afterward. Document any discount and its duration.

- **L20 · Test the offer and objections (P0).** Completion: Present the offer to around five qualified prospects, record their interpretation and objections, and ask for a concrete next step. Revise unclear promises and distinguish compliments from commitments.

## 5 Pricing and unit economics

- **L21 · Choose what customers pay for (P0).** Completion: Select the charging unit: individual, seat, workspace, usage, transaction, or another measurable unit. Match the commercial model to value frequency: subscription, credits, one-time purchase, seasonal pass, or a combination.

- **L22 · Build a delivery cost model (P0).** Completion: Estimate low, typical, and heavy usage costs, including hosting, AI/API calls, storage, payment fees, refunds, and support effort. Separate fixed costs from variable costs and identify loss-making usage.

- **L23 · Test willingness to pay (P0).** Completion: Discuss current spending, budget authority, and realistic alternatives. Present a concrete price and scope, then seek a paid pilot or purchase where appropriate; log objections and commitments.

- **L24 · Set packages and commercial rules (P0).** Completion: Start with one clear paid offer; add tiers only for distinct needs. Specify prices, currency, tax presentation, limits, billing frequency, trial or free access, overages, discounts, cancellation, and refund handling.

- **L25 · Define the pricing review experiment (P1).** Completion: Set a review date, eligible customer group, and decision rules. Track conversion, refunds, usage cost, contribution margin, and repeat use; record uncertainty when the sample is small.

## 6 MVP scope and feasibility

- **L26 · Define the smallest complete workflow (P0).** Completion: Map the minimum journey from user input to the promised useful outcome. Define a measurable activation event and what a successful result looks like.

- **L27 · Test a prototype or manual service (P0).** Completion: Use a prototype, concierge service, or narrow working demo with about five target users. Observe completion and misunderstandings before committing to a larger build.

- **L28 · Separate launch scope from later features (P0).** Completion: Create Must have, Later, and Excluded lists. Tie each launch feature to a customer need and acceptance condition; remove work that does not support the first useful outcome.

- **L29 · Validate architecture and dependencies (P0).** Completion: Choose the necessary stack and integrations. Check third-party permissions, licenses, availability, data flows, costs, and vendor constraints; identify failures that could prevent delivery.

- **L30 · Create the implementation roadmap (P0).** Completion: Turn the launch workflow into small deliverable tasks with dependencies and acceptance criteria. Keep the product brief and architecture decisions aligned with the roadmap.

## 7 Product delivery and service readiness

- **L31 · Implement the core customer journey (P0).** Completion: Deliver access, onboarding, the central workflow, its result, and account settings. Demonstrate the promised outcome with representative inputs and supported devices.

- **L32 · Test quality and first value (P0).** Completion: Observe usability sessions, measure time to first value, and check error handling. For AI features, evaluate a representative case set, failure behavior, quality thresholds, and the customer review step where needed.

- **L33 · Verify permissions and data boundaries (P0).** Completion: Test authentication, authorization, account or tenant isolation, secret handling, export, and deletion as applicable. Resolve defects that could expose or lose customer data.

- **L34 · Prepare operations and recovery (P0).** Completion: Configure monitoring, error alerts, usage and cost controls, backups, and an incident owner. Test recovery and release rollback; define when to pause sign-ups or a failing feature.

- **L35 · Set up customer support (P0).** Completion: Provide a visible support route, onboarding guidance, frequently asked questions, and a response owner. Define achievable response expectations and escalation for billing or service problems.

## 8 Business setup privacy and payments

- **L36 · Confirm the selling entity and tax setup (P0).** Completion: Verify the entity selling the service, customer-facing identity, payment account, invoicing details, and tax treatment for intended markets. Resolve questions with the appropriate professional.

- **L37 · Prepare the applicable terms and privacy documents (P0).** Completion: Match terms, privacy notices, commercial conditions, cancellation, refunds, and any processor agreement to the actual service and target customers. Review French CGU/CGV and consumer requirements where applicable.

- **L38 · Implement data and tracking choices (P0).** Completion: Map collected data, purposes, retention, vendors, access, and deletion. Check the applicable basis and customer notices; configure tracker consent or exemptions appropriately and test the refusal path.

- **L39 · Connect payment to the correct access rights (P0).** Completion: Configure products, prices, checkout or invoicing, receipts, and entitlements. Ensure payment events reliably grant, change, and remove the intended access without duplicate actions.

- **L40 · Test the full billing lifecycle (P0).** Completion: Verify successful and failed payments, retries, duplicate events, renewal or expiry, plan changes, cancellation, refunds, and access changes as relevant. Document how support resolves mismatches.

## 9 Brand website and conversion

- **L41 · Choose a usable name and minimum brand (P1).** Completion: Check the proposed name, domain, and relevant conflicts; reserve the necessary domain when approved. Prepare a simple visual identity and working business email.

- **L42 · Build the landing page (P0).** Completion: Explain the target customer, problem, outcome, product workflow, and next step. Use one primary call to action appropriate to the stage: interview, demo, trial, or purchase.

- **L43 · Publish a clear offer and pricing page (P0).** Completion: Show what is included, limits, billing period, total charge, applicable tax presentation, trial conditions, cancellation, support, and commercial terms. Check consistency with checkout.

- **L44 · Prepare credible product proof (P1).** Completion: Create a short walkthrough and an example result. Use genuine customer quotes or case studies only with permission and supporting evidence; label illustrative or demo data.

- **L45 · Instrument the customer funnel (P0).** Completion: Track acquisition source, sign-up, activation, paid conversion, repeat use, and cancellation or expiry. Test event accuracy and attribution links while minimizing personal data in analytics.

## 10 Acquisition and sales

- **L46 · Select the first acquisition channels (P0).** Completion: Choose one primary channel and one supporting channel based on where the target customer can be reached. Define the hypothesis, time or spend budget, and success condition for each.

- **L47 · Build an initial prospect or recruitment list (P0).** Completion: For B2B, identify qualified accounts and relevant buying roles. For B2C, identify suitable audience sources, communities, or partners. Record relevance and a legitimate contact route.

- **L48 · Set up a simple customer pipeline (P1).** Completion: Track contact, segment, source, stage, next action, owner, expected decision date, and objections. For self-service customers, use lifecycle stages instead of an unnecessary sales process.

- **L49 · Prepare sales and follow-up materials (P1).** Completion: Draft discovery questions, a demo outline, objection responses, and a proposal or pilot agreement when needed. Prepare concise outreach and follow-up drafts adapted to the audience.

- **L50 · Run a small acquisition experiment (P0).** Completion: Launch a bounded test through the selected channels with authorized communications. Track qualified leads, activated customers, payments, time, and spend; choose the next change from the results.

## 11 LinkedIn launch activities

- **L51 · Prepare the founder profile and company Page (P1).** Completion: Update the headline, overview, banner, company information, website link, and next-step invitation so the intended customer immediately understands the problem you solve.

- **L52 · Identify the LinkedIn audience (P1).** Completion: Build a focused list of relevant prospects, partners, and industry voices. Record why they fit and the topic worth discussing; use the list to learn about the audience.

- **L53 · Create a four-week content calendar (P1).** Completion: Draft three useful posts per week as an initial working cadence: a customer problem or insight, practical guidance, and a demo or substantiated result. Give each post one appropriate call to action.

- **L54 · Prepare outreach and the launch announcement (P1).** Completion: Draft personalized research invitations, demo follow-ups, and one launch post with audience, problem, outcome, proof, and link. Engage in relevant conversations and publish or send through the approved workflow.

- **L55 · Measure business outcomes from LinkedIn (P1).** Completion: Use campaign links and pipeline source fields. Review qualified conversations, demos, activated users, and customers alongside impressions; adjust content and follow-up based on customer outcomes.

## 12 Pilot public launch and the first month

- **L56 · Run a small pilot cohort (P0).** Completion: Start with a manageable group, for example 3–5 B2B design partners or 10–20 consumer testers. Record who receives free versus paid access, observe activation, and collect outcome and cost evidence.

- **L57 · Review pilot findings and launch blockers (P0).** Completion: Fix failures that prevent the promised result. Verify customer understanding of the price and limitations; record a go, narrow launch, or hold decision against the readiness gates below.

- **L58 · Execute the public launch runbook (P0).** Completion: Assign publication, customer communication, support coverage, and monitoring. Verify links and checkout, release the approved assets, and use the predefined pause or rollback criteria if needed.

- **L59 · Review activation retention and economics (P0).** Completion: Review cohorts after 7 and 30 days where those periods fit the natural usage cycle. Track first value, repeat use, paying customers, refunds, churn or expiry, delivery cost, and customer feedback.

- **L60 · Choose the next product and growth priorities (P0).** Completion: Rank the main conversion, usability, retention, and cost problems. Select the next three improvements; continue, narrow, or stop acquisition experiments based on evidence before increasing spend.
