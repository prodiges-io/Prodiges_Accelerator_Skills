# Prodiges Startup Review

## Establish the decision
1. Read [working rules](references/evidence-and-working-rules.md).
2. Identify the startup, review period, supplied baseline, current stage, intended decision, and available artifacts. Use [startup brief](assets/startup-brief.template.yaml) only to locate meaningful gaps.
3. Select launch planning, weekly review, milestone review, or authorized cohort review.
4. If a baseline is missing, state the limitation and produce a useful current snapshot. Ask at most three material questions. Do not invent earlier decisions, dates, metrics, or implementation status.

## Assemble the evidence
Read supplied task updates, deliverables, customer notes, experiments, metrics definitions, prior mentor decisions, and constraints. Separate reported facts from artifacts inspected in this run. A screenshot or launch announcement alone does not prove the complete production journey works.
Assign evidence IDs with dates and limitations. Keep zero, missing, not measured, and not applicable distinct.
For cohort work, assess each authorized startup separately and protect its confidential records. Aggregate only for the requested audience.

## Build or adapt the plan
Read [task catalog](references/launch-task-catalog.md) for planning and [milestone gates](references/milestone-gates.md) for readiness.
1. Classify B2B/B2C/mixed, user and payer, current maturity, self-service/assisted sales, recurring/seasonal/occasional value, geography, and operational constraints.
2. Identify the next meaningful milestone. Preserve an approved milestone unless evidence warrants proposing a change.
3. Select a manageable subset of the 60 task templates. Preserve IDs L01–L60 and add justified startup-specific tasks when needed.
4. Adapt each task's owner, deliverable, acceptance criteria, and required evidence. Show approximately three immediate priorities and keep the rest in the backlog.
5. Make dependencies explicit. Permit independent interviews, competitor research, and feasibility work to overlap.
6. Reuse relevant completed work. Do not force a launched startup to repeat discovery without a reason.
7. Include LinkedIn only when audience/channel fit justifies it. Adapt payment and retention work for passes, credits, and seasonal products.
8. Treat counts, dates, budgets, and cadences as planning proposals. Do not impose universal conversion benchmarks or fixed launch durations.

## Review progress
Compare with the supplied baseline: what changed, which claims gained or lost support, completed deliverables, evidence inspected, blockers, and their owners.
Record a founder-reported completion as reported when independent confirmation is unavailable. Keep task status separate from business evidence.
Preserve historical human approvals. When customer, offer, price, limits, architecture, or requirements change, identify stale dependent work and propose proportional revalidation. Do not silently rewrite decisions.

## Review metrics
- Require definition, period, population, denominator, and source for rates.
- Compare like-for-like cohorts. Combine compatible rates from total counts; do not mechanically average percentages.
- Separate free and paid pilots, accounts, users, seats, attempts, and transactions.
- Use a retention interval appropriate to natural product usage. Do not impose daily usage on a seasonal product.
- Keep one-time sales, seasonal revenue, contracted recurring revenue, cash, refunds, and delivery cost distinct. Do not convert a pass into MRR.
- With small or incomparable cohorts, report limited observations and the next useful measurement.
- Treat missing updates as information gaps, not evidence of founder motivation or quality.

## Propose readiness
Use proceed, proceed_with_conditions, hold, or insufficient_evidence as a recommendation.
For each relevant criterion show evidence, assessment, open condition, owner, and timing. Missing evidence means not demonstrated, not automatically broken.
Distinguish accepting a deliverable, running a bounded experiment, serving customers, and demonstrating traction. Do not claim product-market fit, compliance certification, security certification, or human launch approval.
Use the stated scope and risks to decide which conditions materially block the next step.

## Choose the next three actions
Prioritize by decision unblocked, downside of delay, prerequisites, capacity, and learning value. Explain the qualitative reasoning.
For each action include one owner or unassigned, deliverable, dependencies, evidence needed, acceptance criteria, and timing. Return fewer than three if appropriate.
For an experiment specify hypothesis, eligible participants, intervention, observation period, signals, decision rule, owner, and known budget. Label proposed thresholds.

## Produce the handoff
Use [review template](assets/startup-review.template.md), retaining only relevant sections. Lead with recommendation and next milestone.
For cohort mode, show startup, stage, milestone, blocker, mentor decision needed, owner, and next review. Keep private source material restricted to its startup.
Read [output contract](references/output-contract.md) for a saved handoff or requested JSON. Use [report schema](assets/report.schema.json) for structured output. Do not invent a live platform API or claim a save, upload, or acceptance occurred.
Save startup outputs outside the skill package to an authorized destination. Propose shared status changes unless already authorized.
Finish with evidence-reference, dependency, metric-period, capacity, and human-acceptance checks.
